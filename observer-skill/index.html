Observer: a multi-lens reasoning skill for Claude

Observer is a method for reasoning through complex, ambiguous or high-stakes questions from several perspectives at once. Here it is packaged as a skill for Claude. It runs five cognitive lenses, audits any material the person supplies, and ends with a Higher-Order Observer synthesis that gives one recommendation with confidence scores and a transparency report. It comes from the Observer framework for AI-augmented reasoning.

The skill

  • Read version 1.10 of the skill, or get its SKILL.md file from GitHub.
  • An analysis frames the question and runs five lenses: analytical, creative, critical, systems-thinking and pragmatic. Each lens gives a reasoning chain, weighted concepts and a mind map.
  • When the person supplies material, an audit checks it for conflicts, gaps and doubtful claims, quoting it exactly.
  • The synthesis finds where the lenses converge and where they productively diverge, and gives one recommendation with confidence scores and a transparency report.
  • The skill keeps the person in charge. It labels evidence, assumptions and its own knowledge, never invents sources, and names values choices as values choices.

How it was tested and improved

Version 1.10 is the result of ten cycles of self-improvement, run from 21 to 23 October 2026. In each cycle the skill mapped its own process, critiqued it with its own method and revised itself. A blind test on a fixed paper checked seven of the revisions, and the final version was also checked on a held-out text.

  • The audit step, added in the first cycle, raised the test score from 11.5 to an average of 15.4 out of 19 across the seven tested versions. It did so mainly by catching internal inconsistencies.
  • Exact quotation rose from about 90 percent to between 98.7 and 100 percent.
  • On the held-out text, the final version scored 10.5 out of 12, against 10.0 for the original.
  • The later cycles made the output more structured and easier to check, but did not measurably improve detection.

The case-study paper

The test material was an ethics paper on whether AI systems should hold decision authority over medicine, military force and economic allocation. An AI agent wrote it, and it was then revised with the Observer method. Read the paper, or download it as a PDF or a Word file. It is a test object, not a finished paper, and its known defects are recorded in the case study.

Limits

The tests used one paper and one held-out text, both on the same subject. Each version ran once and was scored by one scorer, and every agent was a Claude model, so their agreement is not independent evidence. The skill supports a decision and does not make it. For medical, legal, financial or safety-critical questions, its analysis is not professional advice.
